Why an association needs it

Most quarrels are not about the decision. They are about what happened.

Who is on the voter list? Who was present? Was there a quorum? What was the vote? Which bye-law applied? Where is the paper? Townhall Records answers these before anyone has to ask.

Fewer disputes

What happened is written down, with the date and time. There is less to argue about.

A list that stays correct

The voter list is updated in one place, so everyone works from the same list.

Decisions nobody can quietly change

Once the event ends, the record is published. It cannot be quietly rewritten.

Work done by the software

Check-in, quorum and the count are done by the software, not by hand.

What it covers

Everything an association runs, in one place

Voter list

Manage the list and keep it updated as members change. Works offline, on paper in the room, and online, on the phone.

Attendance and quorum

Members are checked in and the quorum is counted as it happens. Works at the door with paper, or from home on the phone.

Meetings and voting on agenda points

Each agenda point is taken up and voted on, with the result on record. Works in the room, on paper, or online.

Elections and voting as per the local bye-laws

The association's own rules decide how the election runs. Works with paper ballots, on phones, or both.

Offline and online

Paper in the room. Phone from home. Or both in one meeting.

Some members love paper. Some will only use a phone. All of it goes on the same record.

Offline

Members attend and vote on paper, as they always have. Officers enter each ballot into the record, and the count includes them. The paper stays as a second proof.

Online

Members attend and vote on their own phones. Nothing to print, nothing to carry, nothing to count by hand.

Both together

Some members in the room, some at home, in the same meeting or election. One record, one count.

Options that fit most bye-laws

The association sets the rules before the event

Notice, quorum, majorities, proxies, who may vote: these differ from one association and one place to the next. Townhall Records has options that meet the needs of most associations and local bye-laws.

  • The association sets the rules before the event starts.
  • More than 150 regional rule sets to start from.
  • The association's own bye-laws always have the last word.

For every kind of event

Annual and special meetings, committee meetings and elections, for housing societies, professional bodies, clubs and federations.

How much weight a record carries in a dispute depends on the bye-laws and the law where the association is. For a dispute, an advocate should stay in the loop.

Documentation and date and time stamping

Dated. Verified. Impossible to quietly rewrite.

  • Every entry carries the date and time it happened.
  • Verification sits beside the entry: who checked, how, and when.
  • The full record is published when the event ends.
  • Any member can download it and read every step.
  • Once published, it cannot be quietly rewritten.

Voting is secret

A flat is recorded as having voted, never what it voted. Each online voter gets a receipt code that proves the ballot was counted without showing the choice.
